# Some Resources for Graphical Models

I have been taking a break from deep learning, and I am quite into graphical models (GM) lately. So that's why I am gathering resources of understanding various concepts of GM.

Here are some useful courses one can use. They are not sorted/categorized, it's just useful for me to look them through later.

Courses:

Note that except Koller's class, not all of the following classes have video available. 
- - Daphne Koller's [Probabilistic Graphical Models on Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/probabilistic-graphical-models). This is perhaps the best yet the most difficult one. All quiz and exams are filled with trick questions which can challenge even very experienced MLers.
        - The Modern Stanford's version taught by [Stefano Ermon](http://www.cs.stanford.edu/~ermon/) Also check out the [class notes](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228-notes/), which is quite accessible.
        - [The Brown's class](http://cs.brown.edu/courses/cs242/), I found the tutorial lectures are quite useful. It also points to various book chapters for different concepts.
        - [A Short Course on Graphical Models](http://ai.stanford.edu/~paskin/gm-short-course/index.html) by [Mark A. Paskin](http://www.cs.berkeley.edu/~paskin), this is interesting because it's more a short-3 lecture class to cover most basic concepts.

Books: 
- PRML Chapter9
- Koller's "[Probabilistic Graphical Models: Principles and Techniques](https://www.amazon.com/Probabilistic-Graphical-Models-Principles-Computation/dp/0262013193/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1500055570&sr=8-1&keywords=graphical+model)" - fairly dense, but yet again it seems to have the best information.
- Michael Jordan's unfinished book on [Graphical Model](http://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jordan/prelims/).
