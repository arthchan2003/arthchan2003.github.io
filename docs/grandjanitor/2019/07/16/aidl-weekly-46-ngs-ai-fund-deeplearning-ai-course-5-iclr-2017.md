## News

### [The Third Act of Andrew Ng - The 175M AI fund. ](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/30/andrew-ng-officially-launches-his-175m-ai-fund/)

After deeplearning.ai and landing.ai, we finally see the third act of Andrew, which is a 175M AI fund. We first learned about the fund [mid of August](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/15/andrew-ng-is-raising-a-150m-ai-fund/). 

Unlike other VC, AI Fund is similar to Betaworks, which can be seen as an incubator, an accelerator and a fund (see [this article for discussion](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/224446)). Not surprisingly, the first company which The AI Fund is supporting is landing.ai, which Andrew himself created and managed.

One way this could evolve is this fund/incubator ends up effectively being an outsourced AI R&D arm for large companies where spin-outs get acquired by them. We fully expect the fund to return many times its capital.

[techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/30/andrew-ng-officially-launches-his-175m-ai-fund/)

### [The Matrix Calculus You Need For Deep Learning](http://parrt.cs.usfca.edu/doc/matrix-calculus/index.html)

This is a link shared by our members recently on matrix calculus. Matrix calculus is a crucial tool to understand proofs of machine learning techniques, specially for parameter estimation. Yet good text are rare, we can name the [matrix cookbook](http://www2.imm.dtu.dk/pubdb/views/edoc_download.php/3274/pdf/imm3274.pdf) and few others. But these sources do require some preliminary knowledge to read. This text by Terence Parr and Jeremy Howard fills the gap and present a comprehensive overview of the topic. 

[usfca.edu](http://parrt.cs.usfca.edu/doc/matrix-calculus/index.html)

### [The Shallowness of Google Translate](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/01/the-shallowness-of-google-translate/551570/)

This is great piece written by [Douglas Hofstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Hofstadter) on the limitation of current deep-learning-based machine translation, or neural network-based machine translation (NNMT). While he is negative about the technology, he did point out many weaknesses of the current NNMT such as fluency, the word orders. It sounds like interesting research directions for AIDLers in the future. 

[theatlantic.com](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2018/01/the-shallowness-of-google-translate/551570/)
